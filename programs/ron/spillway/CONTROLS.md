# SPILLWAY — control reference

*Your picture is a reservoir. Brightness is water. The slider is the gate.*

## The one-minute version

Set **CREST** so the parts you want to keep are below the waterline. Grab
**FLOODGATE** and pull. Let go and listen to it settle.

## Knobs

**K1 · CREST** — the height of the dam. Fully clockwise, only the hottest
speculars trickle over; roll it back and more and more of the picture is
above the waterline, wanting out. Spill violence is proportional to *head* —
how far above the crest a pixel sits — so this knob decides both *where* the
falls start and *how hard* the bright stuff pours.

**K2 · FLOW** — how much picture converts to water. Low: the image survives,
with delicate bright drips descending from highlights. High: everything over
the crest is swept over the falls and the source drains toward the waterline.

**K3 · PLUNGE** — gravity. Low: syrupy slow-motion spill that dies a short
way down. High: long screaming streaks that visibly accelerate, stretch and
neck down into thin bright filaments all the way to the bottom.

**K4 · CHURN** — aeration. Low: glassy laminar sheets that keep the source's
colour as they fall. High: the water goes white almost immediately — clumpy
boiling whitecaps that build with fall distance.

**K5 · MIST** — impact spray. Wherever falling water lands (the basin, or a
dark ledge it slams into) a soft desaturating fog exhales upward, with
sunlit-droplet sparkle at the top end. Down = dry.

**K6 · DEPTHS** — the water itself: clear (carries the picture's own colour)
→ glacial cyan-white → deep teal-green → murky flood-brown. Tints water,
foam, mist and basin as one palette. The reservoir below the crest is never
tinted — the water sits *inside* your picture.

## Switches

**S7 · GATES** — Weir / Floodgates. Weir: one smooth crest, uniform spill.
Floodgates: the dam splits into 16 gates that haul open and shut on their own
slow schedule (about a second of mechanical travel each) — roaring curtains
next to bone-dry columns.

**S8 · UNDERTOW** — the dark mirror. Shadows below the low waterline climb
*upward* as ragged black tendrils while your highlights fall. The frame
becomes a vertical tug-of-war.

**S9 · BASIN** — plunge pool. Spill volume visibly fills a pool at the bottom
of the frame over seconds; it drains when the falls stop. The surface carries
a rippling, wobbling reflection of everything above it, with a foam crown
where the water lands.

**S10 · SURGE** — scheduled releases. A slow (~6 s) swell heaves extra
discharge over the crest; louder pulses are also whiter. Rides on top of
wherever the slider sits.

**S11 · FREEZE** — the falls ice over (~2 s): motion stops mid-fall, the foam
crystallizes in place, thin icicle glints appear down the streaks, the basin
stills to a mirror. The slider position is captured in the ice. Flip it back
and the thaw takes ~1 s — then the ice lets go with a lurch.

## Slider

**P12 · FLOODGATE** — the reason this program exists.

- **Bottom**: dam closed. Picture intact; a faint shimmer at the waterline
  tells you the pressure is waiting.
- **Pull up**: highlights drip first, then midtones go over, streaks
  lengthen, whitewater builds, the basin starts to fill.
- **Top**: total breach — the whole frame is falling water, your picture
  only readable as colour memory inside the torrent. The last few
  millimetres push into a white-out roar.
- **Let go**: the water settles with real weight — discharge dies over about
  half a second, then a backwash wave re-spills the brightest content about
  a second later, then stillness by two. Hand-fly it: quick pulls, partial
  opens, slow closes all read as heavy water.

CREST and FLOW set the dam's *character*; FLOODGATE is *how far you open it*.
SURGE pulses ride on the slider. FREEZE captures the slider in the ice.
